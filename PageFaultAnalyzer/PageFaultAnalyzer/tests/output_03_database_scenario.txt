--- Interpretation (real-world context) ---
  * A repeated-access workload resembles a database or application that keeps
    returning to a small working set of data pages, with occasional cold lookups.
  * Fault rate of the best practical policy is 55.0%: substantial. More frames
    would probably reduce faults noticeably (see the frame-capacity experiment).
  * LRU had 5 fewer faults than FIFO. Each access refreshes a page's recency, so
    frequently used pages stay resident, while FIFO evicts the oldest-loaded page
    even if it is used all the time.
  * Optimal needed 24 faults; the best practical policy is 9 faults above this
    benchmark. Optimal is theoretical: it needs future knowledge that a real OS
    does not have.
  * Note: this is a page-replacement simulator. Real systems also involve caching,
    working-set tracking, storage latency and allocation policies that are not
    modelled here.
